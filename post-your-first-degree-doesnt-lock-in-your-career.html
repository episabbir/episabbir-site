Your First Degree Doesn't Lock in Your Career

From Nutrition in Bangladesh to Epidemiology in Canada

By Md Sabbir Ahmed · November 10, 2026 · About 2 minutes

Many students worry that their first degree decides their whole career. I did nutrition and food science. Today I am an epidemiologist. Here is how that happened.

Where I started

My path began in Bangladesh. I studied nutrition and food science at Patuakhali Science and Technology University. I finished my bachelor's degree there, and then a master's degree. My master's work focused on WASH, which stands for water, sanitation and hygiene.

Doing research early

While I was still a student in Bangladesh, I won small research fellowships. They came from WaterAid Bangladesh, the Ministry of Science and Technology, and Share-Net Bangladesh. These fellowships supported my early research.

Coming to Canada

In 2021, I started a second master's degree at the University of Saskatchewan (USask) in Saskatoon. My program was Community and Population Health Science. I received a Dean's Scholarship worth $36,000.

This is where I moved from nutrition into epidemiology. Epidemiology is the study of how health problems spread and change in groups of people. During the program, I got travel awards to share my work at conferences in Canada. I also won a best poster award before I finished my degree.

Starting my professional career in Canada

After my degree, I spent a year as a Research Officer at the Saskatchewan Population Health and Evaluation Research Unit (SPHERU) at USask. After that, I became an epidemiologist at the provincial Ministry of Health in Saskatchewan. Today I work on childhood immunization surveillance.

What helped me

If you are thinking about a similar path, here is what helped me:

  • Start research during your first degree.
  • Apply for small grants and fellowships. A small one still counts on your record.
  • Look for scholarships when you apply to a graduate program.
  • Share your work at conferences, even as a student.

The takeaway

Your first degree is a starting point. The research and data skills you build along the way can take you into a new field. Mine took me from food and nutrition to vaccines.
